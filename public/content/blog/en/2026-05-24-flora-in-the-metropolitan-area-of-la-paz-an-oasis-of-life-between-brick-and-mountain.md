The city of La Paz unfolds commandingly within a unique mountain basin: a dramatic geological fracture descending from the vastness of the Bolivian Altiplano at 4,000 meters above sea level, following the ancient course of underground rivers carved into steep hillsides. Descending into the valley, the historic center rests at 3,600 meters, while the urban fabric stretches down to residential neighborhoods at 3,200 meters of elevation. The iconic exposed brick buildings climbing the slopes bestow an unmistakable personality upon our city. However, rapid demographic growth has raised dense concrete blocks along the main avenues, where private courtyards have been gradually replaced, concentrating the urban landscape where the fertile green valley on the outskirts of ancient *Chuquiago Marka* once flourished.

Despite this dense urban crust, valuable surrounding green spaces survive today, protected by locals with deep pride and environmental awareness. Among these natural oases, the majestic **Pura Pura Woods**, the peaceful **Aranjuez Valley**, the green lung of the **Central Urban Park**, the biological reserve of the **Auquisamaña Woods**, and the mythical clay spires of the **Valley of the Souls** (*Valle de las Ánimas*) stand out.

In these sanctuaries, visitors can appreciate the rich indigenous biodiversity of the Andes alongside foreign species that, brought from distant lands, successfully adapted to the extreme altitude. Such is the case of the eucalyptus, a tree native to the other side of the world that now outlines our visual horizon.

### A Tapestry of Colors: Flowers and Cacti of La Paz

The soil of La Paz bursts into vibrant color through wild and ornamental flowers of striking beauty. Among them, geraniums, colorful pelargoniums, elegant calla lilies, stately acanthus, subtle snapdragons, calendula, firecracker plants, and wild roses stand out. Special mention goes to the wild tarwi (lupine) and, of course, the sacred **kantuta**—the national flower whose brilliant tricolor hues narrate the history of our Andean heritage. Guarding the arid slopes, the landscape is punctuated by striking cacti such as the prickly pear (tuna), tunilla, and ancient agaves, along with various endemic cacti varieties that seem to sculpt the surrounding mountains.

### Shrubs, Aromas, and Green Giants

The shrub layer is dominated by the vibrant Spanish broom (retama)—which fills the air with its sweet fragrance and intense yellow hue—various types of privet, and delicate acacia. Emerging from the plant textures are resilient climbers and trailers like the canary bird bush (pajarilla), wild tobacco, and evergreen ivy, which tenaciously embraces and enlivens the city's ancient stone walls. The undergrowth and grounds are carpeted in freshness by a dense layer of *chiji* (a highly resilient native grass) and white clover, interspersed with the aromatic notes of medicinal herbs like lemon verbena (*cedrón*) and lavender.

At higher elevations, the trees tell their own story of adaptation: the ancestral **queñua** (*Polylepis*), capable of withstanding the freezing winds of the high plateau; weeping willows guardedly weeping over riverbanks; slender poplars; and robust cypresses and pines. Lower down in the Southern Zone (Zona Sur) of La Paz, where the microclimate becomes noticeably warmer and more generous, the landscape surprises with the presence of exotic palm trees. Dominating the heights, the eucalyptus—introduced to Bolivia in the early 20th century—stands as an adopted species that took such deep root it is now an inseparable part of La Paz's landscape and identity.

### Green Routes for the Conscious Traveler

For those seeking a peaceful respite amidst the urban hustle, the **La Paz Botanical Garden** in the central Miraflores neighborhood offers a charming walk to encounter each of these species up close. If you have more time and love trekking, the breathtaking formations of the **Valley of the Souls** and the cactus-lined trails surrounding the famous **Valley of the Moon** will provide unforgettable prehistoric vistas.

Likewise, for birdwatchers and wildlife enthusiasts, the secluded ecosystem of the **Auquisamaña Woods** offers a magical trail, untouched by mass tourism, boasting one of the most stunning and comprehensive panoramic views of the entire Southern Zone.
